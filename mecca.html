mecca

Makkah Al-Mukarramah is the Qibla of Muslims and the holiest place on Earth for the Islamic nation,
and it is known as "Umm al-Qura" (Mother of Cities).


It is considered the cradle of the Muhammadan Message and a destination toward which hearts from every corner of the globe turn daily; it embraces the Sacred House and instills a sense of serenity and awe in the souls of its visitors.
Today, it continues to showcase magnificent architecture and exceptional services, welcoming millions of Hajj and Umrah pilgrims with ease and peace of mind.

  • Location and Geography

    • Location:
      Situated in the Hejaz region of western Saudi Arabia.
    • Terrain:
      Located in a valley surrounded by the rugged Sarawat Mountains; it lies approximately 70 km from the city of Jeddah and the Red Sea coast.

  • Religious and Spiritual Significance

    • The Qibla (Direction of Prayer):
      It houses the Sacred Mosque (Al-Masjid al-Haram), at the center of which stands the Holy Kaaba—the focal point toward which Muslims across the globe turn during their daily prayers.
    • The Cradle of Islam:
      It witnessed the birth of the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) and the revelation of the first verses of the Holy Quran to him in the Cave of Hira.

  • Key Religious and Historical Landmarks

    • Al-Masjid al-Haram and the Kaaba:
      The greatest mosque in Islam; the Holy Kaaba is considered the first House established for mankind on Earth, according to Islamic belief.
    • Cave of Hira (Jabal al-Nour):
      The cave where the Prophet (peace be upon him) used to worship and where the Revelation first descended upon him. Cave of Thawr: The cave where the Prophet (peace be upon him) and his companion, Abu Bakr al-Siddiq, took refuge during the migration to Medina.
    • The Holy Sites:
      These include Mina, Muzdalifah, and Arafat; the majority of Hajj rituals are performed there. Zamzam Well: The blessed well located within Al-Masjid al-Haram, near the Holy Kaaba.

    Modern Mecca

  • The city has undergone massive urban development to accommodate the growing numbers of Hajj and Umrah pilgrims, and is distinguished by the presence of mega-infrastructure projects, such as:
    • Expansion of the Grand Mosque:
      The largest historical expansion to accommodate millions of worshippers.
    • Abraj Al-Bait Complex (Makkah Clock Tower):
      One of the world's tallest buildings, overlooking the Grand Mosque in Makkah.
    • Haramain High-Speed Railway:
      Connects Makkah, Madinah, and Jeddah to facilitate visitor travel.
