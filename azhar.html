al-azhar al-sharif

Al-Azhar Al-Sharif is considered the largest Islamic religious and scholarly institution in the world; it stands as one of Cairo’s most significant landmarks and a preeminent symbol of Islamic civilization. Its history spans more than a thousand years, encompassing worship, education, and social and national guidance.

  • Founding and Naming

    • Origins:
      The Fatimid commander Jawhar al-Siqilli began the construction of Al-Azhar Mosque in 359 AH / 970 AD, under the commission of the Fatimid Caliph al-Mu'izz li-Din Allah. The first Friday prayer was held there in 972 AD.

    • Naming:
      It is widely believed that the mosque was named "Al-Azhar" in honor of—and as a tribute to—Lady Fatimah al-Zahra (may Allah be pleased with her).

  • Historical Development


    • Throughout its history, Al-Azhar has undergone several major transformations:

      The Fatimid Era:

      It began as a congregational mosque and a university dedicated to disseminating the teachings of the Ahl al-Bayt (the Prophet’s family) and promoting the Shi'a school of thought.

    • The Ayyubid Era:
      Saladin (Salah al-Din al-Ayyubi) suspended sermons at the mosque for a period and encouraged Sunni scholarship. Consequently, Al-Azhar gradually transitioned toward teaching Sunni jurisprudence and the four Sunni schools of thought (madhahib).

    • The Mamluk Era:
      This period marked the "Golden Age" of Al-Azhar; its physical structure was expanded, and it attracted prominent scholars and thinkers (such as Ibn Khaldun, Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani, and Al-Firuzabadi).

    • The Modern Era:
      The office of the Grand Imam (Mashyakhat al-Azhar) was formally established, with Sheikh Muhammad al-Kharashi serving as the first Grand Imam in 1690 AD. In 1961, a law was enacted to reform Al-Azhar, leading to the integration of secular, practical faculties—such as medicine and engineering—alongside the existing faculties of Islamic studies and Arabic language.

  • Structure and Key Sectors of Al-Azhar


    • Today, Al-Azhar Al-Sharif consists of several primary bodies that manage its scholarly and organizational activities:
      The Mashyakhat Al-Azhar (Office of the Grand Imam): The institution’s supreme leadership, headed by the Grand Imam (Sheikh of Al-Azhar).

    • Al-Azhar University: The third-oldest university in the world, comprising dozens of faculties covering Islamic studies, medicine, engineering, and the humanities.


    • The Council of Senior Scholars: The highest jurisprudential and scholarly authority within Al-Azhar, responsible for determining positions on emerging issues.


    • The Islamic Research Academy: Tasked with disseminating Islamic culture, renewing religious thought, reviewing publications, and overseeing educational missions.


    • The Al-Azhar Institutes Sector: Responsible for supervising pre-university education (primary, preparatory, and secondary levels) across all of Egypt’s governorates.

  • The Al-Azhar Curriculum

    • The Al-Azhar curriculum is characterized by moderation and balance, and it consists of three scholarly pillars:

    • Doctrine (‘Aqidah): The study of the creed of Ahl al-Sunnah wa al-Jama‘ah (the Ash‘ari and Maturidi schools).

    • Jurisprudence (Fiqh): A pluralistic approach involving the comparative study of the four Sunni schools of law (Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi‘i, and Hanbali).

    • Spiritual Conduct (Suluk): The purification of the soul and the cultivation of ethics, following the path of moderate, Sunni Sufism.

  • National and Humanitarian Role

    • Resisting Occupation: Al-Azhar served as a command center for popular uprisings in Egypt—both against the French campaign (the first and second Cairo revolts) and against the British occupation.

    • International Students: Each year, Al-Azhar welcomes tens of thousands of students from over 100 countries, providing them with free instruction in Islamic studies and the Arabic language.

    • Global Dialogue:
      Al-Azhar plays a prominent role in promoting world peace and fostering dialogue among different religions and cultures.
